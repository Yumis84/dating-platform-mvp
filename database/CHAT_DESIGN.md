# Chat design — Anonymous chat module

Overview
--------
This document describes the design of the anonymous chat module for the Dating Platform MVP. The chat connects a male user with a female profile owner without exposing personal contact information. Communication is relayed via the platform (Telegram bot / WebApp) so participants remain anonymous until they choose to reveal contact details outside the platform (out of scope).

Purpose
-------
- Provide a safe, anonymous messaging channel between a man and an owner of a woman profile.
- Minimize PII exposure and enforce moderation and safety rules.
- Allow blocking and reporting flows and keep auditable records.

Relationships and entities
--------------------------
- chat_sessions — session between two users (initiator and respondent), linked to a profile (female profile) and optionally to initiating context (catalog view, match, etc.).
- messages — stored message history for the session; delivered via platform proxies (Telegram bot / WebApp) so participants remain anonymous.
- chat_blocks — records of who blocked whom and why.
- chat_reports — reports/complaints filed by participants or moderators.

Anonymity principle
-------------------
- The chat is mediated by the platform. The male participant sees the public profile information of the female profile (display_name, age, avatar, description) but never the female user's Telegram id or phone/email.
- The female participant receives messages from an anonymous sender (alias) and sees the male's profile (if available) within limits defined by privacy rules; the platform may show an alias (e.g., "Пользователь 2543") and minimal metadata (age range, city) but never direct contact details.
- Messages relay only content and metadata necessary for delivery (sender alias, timestamp). Telegram file_ids may be used to deliver photos but raw Telegram IDs are not exposed to the counterpart.

What each party sees
--------------------
- Man:
  - Female profile public fields: display_name (if allowed), age, city, avatar_file_id, short description.
  - Messages he sends and receives (text, allowed media via Telegram file_id proxies).
- Woman:
  - Male alias (generated by platform), limited profile metadata (age range, city) — not the user's Telegram id or contact info.
  - Messages she sends and receives.

Data never transmitted to the counterpart
----------------------------------------
- Telegram user IDs, internal user UUIDs, email addresses, phone numbers and other contact details.
- Payment data, billing info, or verification documents.

Blocking and moderation
-----------------------
- If participant A blocks participant B, a record is created in chat_blocks with who blocked whom and reason; the session may be set to status = 'BLOCKED' and further messages from B will be rejected.
- Reports (chat_reports) can be filed by either participant; reports include reason, optional comment, and attached moderation metadata. Reports feed moderation workflows (AI/ADMIN) for review.
- Repeated abuse can result in account-level actions (suspension), recorded in audit_events (admin task).

Rate limits and anti-abuse
-------------------------
- Implement rate limits per user and per session (e.g., X messages per minute, Y messages per day). Violations create audit_events and may temporarily restrict messaging.
- Limit media submissions (photos) per session per day to avoid spam/abuse.
- Use heuristics and AI moderation to detect spam/fraud patterns (separate module WF_04). Excessive messaging from new accounts may trigger throttling.

Message history and retention
-----------------------------
- Messages are stored in messages table with created_at timestamps. Message content retention policy must be defined (e.g., retain 180 days then archive) to limit storage and comply with privacy.
- chat_sessions keep summary metadata and status; inactive sessions may be archived after a retention period.
- chat_reports and chat_blocks stored indefinitely for compliance, but consider archiving older records to cold storage.

Privacy and security considerations
-----------------------------------
- Access controls: only authorized services and admins can read raw messages. Moderators should have an audit trail for access.
- Encrypt sensitive fields at rest if required by policy (message text might be sensitive). Consider field-level encryption for messages.
- Do not expose Telegram file_id or other platform-specific identifiers to the counterpart; use the platform to fetch and deliver media.

Notes and future work
---------------------
- Integration with moderation workflows (AI moderation and Admin Panel) to automatically flag/close sessions based on content.
- Optional ephemeral messaging mode (messages auto-delete after X time) — design required later.
- Rate-limited free-tier vs. paid features (out of scope until payments module is designed).
